Assumptions, risk and growth

Key assumptions

  • Average doctor hourly rate: ₹400 (range ₹250–₹700 based on performance)
  • Panchakarma utilization: 70% (Y1) → 100% (Y3+)
  • Medicine sales markup: 100% on inventory cost
  • Consultation volume: 12/day/doctor at 75% utilization
  • Cost inflation: 4–5% annually from Year 2
  • Depreciation: straight-line over 10 years

Risk mitigation

  • High EBITDA margin (51%) provides buffer against lower utilization
  • Multiple revenue streams reduce dependency on a single service
  • Medicine sales provide consistent recurring revenue
  • Surplus cash flow allows reinvestment and expansion

Sensitivity

  • Even at 50% utilization, the venture generates ₹9.24 Cr cash flow (covers 3.2× investor payout)
  • Break-even per centre: ₹4.16 lakh/month (currently at ₹8.56 lakh)
  • Margin of safety: 106% above break-even

Sustainability

  • Self-sustaining from Month 1 (surplus ₹11.30 Cr in Year 1)
  • No additional capital required after initial investment
  • Positive cash flow in all 5 years
  • Cumulative surplus of ₹106.80 Cr by Year 5 for expansion

Scalability

  • Year 4–5: open 10–15 additional centres using retained surplus
  • Franchise model possible from Year 3 (brand established)
  • Online medicine sales channel (additional revenue stream)
  • Corporate wellness packages (B2B expansion)

The venture is highly viable and sustainable

  • Investor gets guaranteed 23% annual return (₹2.88 Cr/year)
  • Company retains ₹106.80 Cr over 5 years for growth
  • Break-even achieved immediately in Year 1
  • Multiple self-sustaining revenue streams
  • 51% EBITDA margin provides strong operational buffer
  • Scalable model with surplus reinvestment capacity

Recommendation: proceed with the venture. The model comfortably supports the 23% investor return while generating substantial company surplus for expansion and long-term wealth creation.
